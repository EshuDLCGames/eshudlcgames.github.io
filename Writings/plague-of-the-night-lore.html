Chapter I

The Curse and the Ritual

✠

Twelve thousand lives will lift the curse. A thousand must be given every month for one year, and with each month's offering one curse is lifted, until there are no more curses and all twelve thousand have been paid.

That is only the first year. If the ritual is performed a second time, the curses are not merely lifted but healed. No hunger. No silver. No sun to fear. The gifts become gifts again, as they were when Bloodlust first tore them from his own body.

The Rebirth of the Maker

The vampires call it the Ry-Rythullda, a legend Nia and Veric heard from their nan as children. The Maker's tomb has been found. The sacrifices will wake him, and when he wakes, a new age of darkness will reign.

The Silver Sarcophagus

Bloodlust's beast half lies inside: the lion's head, the sword claws, every horror he ever was. It is sealed with twelve seals, one for each gift. Because their father lies in silver, every cursed creature burns at its touch.

Mathias tells his court that the children of light sealed it. The truth is that Bloodlust's own Scarred Priests laid the demon there, at his command.

The Silver Coffin

Do not mistake one for the other. The long box an old man drags through the streets on a rope is a different coffin entirely. Vampires flee from it, and he will not say what is inside.

Chapter II

The King's Orders

✠

Fear and surprise are our greatest weapons, and we must use them wisely. Let some escape, just so they are haunted by the fear of what is coming. So reads the plan of attack, written in the king's own hand, in four stages.

  1. I

    Take out every military threat

    Before the attack, the Shadow Fangs eliminate or infect the navy and military outposts in New York, then set fire to the docks. There shall be no travel by sea. Cutting all communication is critical to the mission.

  2. II

    Tunnels and airports

    Veric and Gan take their troops and attack the tunnels and airports. The flow of traffic must stop. No one enters or leaves the city.

  3. III

    The subways

    We have complete control of the subway system. On the king's signal, every train stops and every gate locks. Those on the trains and underground shall never see the surface again. They shall be our reinforcements.

  4. IV

    The streets of New York

    When stages one, two and three are complete, we pour into the streets. Kill anyone who tries to escape the city. Change all that you kill.

Chapter III

The Dome

✠

Magnus cast it a little after sunset on the first day, a Monday. People on the bridges saw it first: a darkness folding down out of the sky, smooth as glass, swallowing the stars.

From outside, it is a vast black sphere, smooth and shining, that nothing can pierce. From inside, it is a wall of fog and shadow. Walk into it on the west side of the city and you will wander through the grey until you find yourself walking back in on the east side.

The power is out. Dusk and dawn blend together into one seamless night, and no one gets out.

Magnus, the Cursebreaker

Mathias's sorcerer. He sealed the city and is bound to break the twelve curses of man and raise the dead.

Chapter IV

The Scarred Priests

✠

They were Bloodlust's second twelve. The first twelve had been given gifts; these were given the means to destroy them.

He fed each priest his blood, and runes rose across their skin, one marking for every curse. Each priest bore one mark above all others, a mark that made him death to a single curse. A priest's bare hand could kill. Anything blessed with a priest's blood, a blade, a bell or a bullet, could kill as well.

When they marched, the Twelve Empires fell. In time their followers founded the orders of vampire hunters and wolf hunters. Of the twelve, only one still lives.

The priests made one mistake: their blood is a weapon, and weapons can be taken. Nia carries it in small glass vials.

Chapter V

The Freeing of the Wolves

✠

Long ago, the vampires ruled over the werewolves and the other ten curses as slave masters, forcing them to do as they wished. The wolves killed and infected for them, and became the monsters of legend.

Then a human and a half-blood stood up to the vampire king. With the help of Hugo's parents, they overthrew vampire rule and set the wolves free to live their own way.

Hugo was one of the last pure werewolves ever born, before the curse. If a Pure Blood wakes the Maker, she believes the vampire empire will rise again, and her people will be slaves once more.

On the werewolf's bite

One bitten by a werewolf lives ten times as long as any human, grows stronger and faster, and gains heightened senses and reflexes. The change comes in a nightmare, in a single night. And yes, they will have to kill.

Chapter VI

Blades, Blood and Silver

✠

The Midnight Blade

Borne by Nia, on her hip.

Simple and slightly curved, with a handle of polished silver and a blade so black it reflects no light at all. Its scabbard is made from the skin of something so ancient no one knows what it was. Its wielder's heart must be empty, and the blade has a cost Nia has already paid once. She won it from Lord Magnis more than 500 years ago.

The Hungry Blade

Carried by Nia, sealed in a tube case on her back. Never drawn.

Forged by the swordsmith Hammru in exchange for the souls of himself and everyone he knew. Three and a half feet of strange silver metal that sparkles like diamond dust, its handle wrapped in black silk, its guard marked with a dragon and a phoenix biting each other's tails. It slaughtered Hammru's village, then the noble's family, then the General who wielded it. It is still searching for a worthy master. See Chapter VII.

Vials of Priest's Blood

Borne by Nia.

Bled from every Scarred Priest she has killed. With it she blesses her blades and her bullets.

The Twelve-Mark Dagger and Twin Pistols

Borne by Hugo.

A short, plain, enchanted dagger, marked along the blade with the twelve symbols of the Scarred monks. With it, twin customized Glock 19s loaded with enchanted bullets. Hugo can mark other people's bullets too, making them far more effective against vampires.

Silver Gauntlets

Worn by Beast.

Polished for fun behind the counter of a gothic hobby shop in the East Village, until the night they were needed.

Runic Marks

Carried by Father Anselm.

They cover his skin and glow a soft, pale light when the dead are near. Vampires shrink back from it, the way a hand pulls back from a stove. The light does not last.

Chapter VII

The Hungry Blade

✠

The opening pages of this tale are lost. It begins again after the sword was finished.

He never left his home, and no one came to visit him. He became a hermit, absorbed in his work; it had become his life. The day he finished it, he slept for what seemed like an eternity. When he woke, it was to a nightmare. There was blood all over his clothes, his hands and his face. The sword wasn't on the shelf where he had left it. Instead it stood buried four inches into the ground, like the sword in the stone.

Confused and frightened, he got out of bed and cleaned himself off. As he dressed, he noticed that all his windows and mirrors were broken. When he tried to open the front door, it stuck at first, as if something were blocking it from the other side. With a little effort he forced it open. What he saw on the other side was horrific: an alley littered with dead bodies, all cut down with deadly accuracy. Not just any bodies. These were the town's men and women. He ran.

He ran from house to house, from store to store, even to the old rundown pubs at the edge of town, the places mothers warn their sons never to visit. They were all dead. All of them. The entire village had been slaughtered. But by whom, and why? The streets were riddled with bodies; there must have been a huge fight. Whoever went on this killing spree must have started here, because the sheriff and his two deputies lay dead by the fountain on Main Street. The sheriff was missing his head, and his deputies had been gutted like sows.

Bodies lay criss-crossed over each other, and they weren't alone, not by a long shot. There were women and children as well. Most of them lay face down, with gaping wounds across their backs. They had been killed while running away.

As he walked up to one child, he saw it was his neighbor. He had watched this boy grow up. His parents had been killed two years after his birth. He was a bastard child, looked down upon by the rest of the village, but Hammru liked him. The boy would sometimes bring him water from the springs to cool the heated steel.

The thought jostled something loose. He could see the boy running in fear, terror filling his face like a storm. He saw him trip, and bleed an ocean of blood, so red it stained the ground where he fell. Hammru looked at the boy again and saw the stain beside him. It had dried almost black on the dirt, but his face still wore the same look of complete terror.

When he could stand no more, he returned home, walking over the bodies in his yard. The yard wasn't only paved with corpses, but with weapons as well: axes, bows, knives, cookware, shovels and swords. He even recognized one as his own. It was one of his first, and he had given it to the sheriff's son on his fifteenth birthday. Next to the sword lay the lifeless form of that young man, not fifteen anymore, and no longer full of life.

Climbing over the bodies, he opened his front door. And there, standing less than a foot from his blood-stained bed, was the blade. Even among all this gore it was beautiful, the most beautiful thing he had ever seen. It shone in the afternoon sun like a crystal, scattering light in every color. The sword was perfect, and for a moment he forgot the outside world: the carnage at his doorstep, the stains on his sheets, even the smell of old blood that filled his house. There was only the blade.

It was three and a half feet from tip to end, made entirely of the strange metal he had found, every inch of it, even the handle. The handle was wrapped in black silk, bound tight with a thin rope that formed an X pattern on both sides. The metal was a brilliant silver that sparkled as if it had been dusted with diamonds. Even the guard was made of it. The guard was small, only half an inch out from the blade, and it bore the sign of a dragon and a phoenix: the dragon's jaws closed tight around the phoenix's tail, while the phoenix did the same to the dragon.

It was amazing just to look at. It was euphoric. This was the sword. It stood untarnished by the killing; the only sign at all was a stain on the floor where the blade rested. Perfect, like his love. This was, without a doubt, the sword that would win her hand.

In his heart he knew what had happened to the village, though he never once said it, even to himself. It's amazing the things love can make you ignore. The next day he gathered all the bodies, laid them in the temple on the hill, and burned the building to ash. In the back of his mind he heard his own voice: The souls of myself and everyone I know. He hoped this would be the end of his deal.

He was wrong.

The audience

That evening, while the blaze still burned bright, he traveled back to Okinawa.

When he arrived, he did not visit his family. He went straight to the noble's estate and asked for an audience. It was granted, for he was a great swordsmith, and the General himself carried one of his creations. The audience was brief. In the room were the General and five of his men, the noble and his wife. The noble's daughter was not there, and Hammru wondered at it as he presented the sword. When it was unsheathed, the room, already silent, seemed emptied of all sound. Everyone held their breath.

The noble was stunned. "To what do I owe the honor of such an amazing gift?"

Hammru, on his knees, head bowed, arms held out before him, smiled. "I wish to ask for your daughter's hand in marriage."

There was another pause, a few chuckles, and then the noble answered, with sadness and pity in his voice. "I'm sorry to tell you, but my daughter was wed six months ago. She lives now with her husband, on the outskirts of town."

Hammru's hands began to shake, and the sword wobbled. He didn't truly hear the words, but he felt them all the same. A thousand daggers flew from the noble's mouth into his chest. His mouth went dry, and he felt he would vomit up first his heart, and then every organ in his body.

The noble saw his trembling hands. "Please accept my apologies, and know this: your sword is the finest I have ever seen, and I would be honored to have it in my collection. But I cannot accept it."

He was about to go on when the sword fell to the floor, and Hammru began to weep. As he wept, he spoke.

"The sword was forged for a great man. It is too heavy for me. I do not wish to bear its weight any longer."

And with that, he rose, turned and walked out, leaving the sword behind.

Truth and Justice

He believed he would never sleep that night, but he did, and well. The next day was a whirlwind. Hammru woke to the sound of swords leaving their sheaths, and steel at his face. His room was full of soldiers, every weapon already drawn. He was an expert swordsman, but a better smith, and he was unarmed.

The General stood before him, wild-eyed. "Where is the sword?!"

Hammru truly had no idea what he meant. The only sword he had traveled with was the one he had left with the noble. "I don't know what you're talking about!" he answered, in anger and fear.

"Take him, and search the room." The General seized him as he spoke. "You will pay for your heinous crime."

And in that instant it all came back: the village, the children, everything. But it still made no sense. How could they have known?

"We found it, sir," said one of the soldiers.

"Good. Bring it, and him, to the town center." The General looked into Hammru's face with hatred and disgust, and spat in it.

The town center was what you would expect at the heart of a city. At least a hundred people had gathered around a wooden stage six feet high, with one set of steps at the rear and a great post at either end. The right-hand post bore the word TRUTH; the left, JUSTICE. What shocked Hammru was the banner strung between them: In memory of our beloved noble family. What was going on? He had seen the noble and his wife only yesterday.

My soul, and the souls of everyone I know. His own voice repeated over and over in his head.

They put him on his knees before a small wooden table, his hands bound behind his back. He wanted desperately to shout his innocence. But the image of the boy, lying dead in a stain of his own blood with terror on his face, kept replaying in his mind. He was silent.

The General looked at Hammru, and then at the crowd. "The execution begins now! For the murders of our beloved nobleman and his family, I sentence you to death by the blade."

The crowd cheered, louder and longer than before. The General turned to him. "Have you any last words?"

Hammru looked up at the General, and then out at the crowd, and what he saw both saddened and enraged him. In the middle of the crowd stood his lovely lady, her arms wrapped tight around her husband. What sickened him most was that they looked like they were in love. He spoke his last.

"I cannot atone for sins I have no memory of. But if it was I, and I believe it was, then I accept your judgment. But know this: the sword is hungry, and it will keep feeding until it finds a worthy master."

The General, unmoved, drew the sword from its sheath. It shone like a diamond in the noon sun, and for a moment it blinded the crowd. That was a mercy, for it hid the look that crossed the General's face: madness, power and greed, man in his purest state. As the shine died down, he held the blade high above the crowd.

"This sword has claimed the lives of thirty-seven innocent people. He says it is evil, but that is not true. The evil that drove this sword to murder kneels here before you, and today this sword shall be freed from this demon, and there will be peace in the land forever."

And with that, he raised the blade above Hammru's neck, and swung, and then...

All was dark.

Three months later, the General was executed by the same blade, for the murders of the nobleman's daughter, her husband and his entire family. The sword passed to another, who met the same fate. It has wandered the land ever since, searching for a master, leaving carnage in its wake.

It is known as the Hungry Blade. Today it rests in a tube case on Nia's back. She has never drawn it.

Chapter VIII

A Gazetteer of the Dark City

✠

The A Train, West 4th Street

For two weeks, riders vanished from its late cars. Detective Gordon Lesher was aboard when it stopped dead in the tunnel and the lights went out.

The Tunnels

Every gate is chained and locked. Gordon and Thomas ran here until a stray monk found them, and Nia cut the chain from the other side.

A Gothic Hobby Shop, East Village

Black-painted windows, silver bells over the door, skull candles and comic books. Four young vampires attacked here in the open, under the lights. Lexi died behind the register.

42nd and Broadway, Times Square

Where Karl and Lark first saw the empty sky. The Savage killed Lark here in seconds.

An Alley

Nia liked New York alleys, because people leave you alone there. At least ten fledglings ambushed her in one, organized as they had not been since the old days.

The Bridge

Where Nagoomo nearly lost his arm, and Hugo changed to save him.

Wazier's Bodega

Its gate held by a double padlock, until Hugo tore it off and twisted it shut again with her bare hands. Wazier's family sheltered here from sunset on.

The Church Downtown

A radio call promised food, water, guns and safety, and the survivors came. Veric let them gather. Then he sent the Shadow Fangs.
